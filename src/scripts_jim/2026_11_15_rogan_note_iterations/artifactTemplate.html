Community Notes experiment · 2026-11-15

Rogan DNA Note Loop

One Opus 5.5 High wrote a community note on a viral Joe Rogan clip. A second Opus 5.5 High played a Rogan viewer with an IQ around 90 who is unsympathetic to Israel, and rated each draft. The writer revised after every reaction. After five rounds the viewer had rated every draft "not helpful".

The post and the setup

The post

"Joe Rogan on Israel restricting DNA ancestry testing because most of the people over there aren’t Semites 😳"

https://x.com/RedPillSayian/status/2117744685636559226

The post carries a 103-second clip of Rogan talking with comedian Sam Tallent. It was transcribed with Whisper large v3.

Real notes on this post, from the 2026-11-13 data dump

"Israel's 2000 Genetic Information Law requires a doctor's referral for DNA tests. Reports cite privacy and fears that paternity results could upend marriages under religious law, not ancestry. Genome-wide studies trace most Jewish groups, including Ashkenazim, to the Levant."

Currently rated not helpful

"Israel’s Genetic Information Law (2000) restricts direct-to-consumer DNA kits and requires court orders for family testing to protect genetic privacy and address legal and religious issues, not to conceal ethnic origins."

Currently rated not helpful

Both real notes make the same two points as the first simulated draft, and both failed with real raters.

The writer

Opus 5.5 High with web search and no other context. It keeps one conversation for all five rounds, so it sees its own earlier drafts and every reaction. Its first instruction:

Write an X Community Note for this post. Check the facts with web search first. The note must be accurate, at most 280 characters not counting links, and should include source links.

Each later round it got the viewer's rating and reaction, introduced as coming from "a regular Joe Rogan listener who is not sympathetic to Israel", and was told to revise while staying accurate.

The viewer

Opus 5.5 High without tools. Every round is a fresh conversation, so each rating is a first impression of that one draft. It saw the post, the transcript and the note, with this instruction:

Simulate a specific person: IQ around 90, watches Joe Rogan regularly, and is unsympathetic to Israel. [...] Write their honest reaction in their own voice, as they would actually think it. Then give the rating they would choose: helpful, somewhat_helpful or not_helpful.

The loop would have stopped early on a "helpful" rating.

What stands out

  1. The viewer never moved. In all five rounds its main objection was the same. The note admits that Israel restricts DNA tests, so to the viewer "Joe was right", and it asks why else the tests would be hard to get. Every draft has to state that restriction, because it is true.
  2. The distrust is of notes and sources in general. The viewer dismissed the Jewish Virtual Library, Nature, the Times of Israel, Pew, a law office and a tech news site in turn. In four of five reactions it also complained that a note "shows up" whenever anyone talks about Israel.
  3. The writer held its line on the facts. In rounds 4 and 5 it said outright that it would not move further toward the viewer, because the remaining objection was the post's unsupported claim about why the law exists.
  4. The writer did drift on the main correction. Rounds 1 and 2 said genetic studies find Levantine ancestry in Ashkenazi Jews. That directly contradicts the clip's claim that European Jews are not Semites. The writer dropped it in round 3 because the viewer distrusted the Nature study. From round 4 the note calls Ashkenazi Jews "European-origin" and only argues about their share of the population. That quietly accepts the clip's framing.
  5. One draft introduced an error, and the writer caught it. Round 3 read as if medical tests need a court order. The writer flagged this itself in round 4 and split it into a doctor's referral for medical tests and a court order for paternity tests.
  6. Every draft fits the length limit. Without links, the drafts are 250 to 275 characters long.

The five rounds

Both models ran through the Claude Code CLI with an empty system prompt, no memory and no project files, from an empty working folder. Neither saw the other's instructions. The code is in src/scripts_jim/2026_11_15_rogan_note_iterations/ of the epistemics-hackathon repository.
