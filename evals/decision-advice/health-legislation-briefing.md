Bill Details
1. Community Health Center Expansion Act
Sponsor: Sen. Klobuchar (D-MN) / Sen. Collins (R-ME)
Status: Introduced; referred to HELP. Companion bill in the House has 34 co-sponsors.

Authorises $5.8B over five years to expand the Community Health Center program, with targeted funding for areas designated as primary care deserts. Includes provisions for capital grants to build new facilities in underserved rural and urban areas, and extends the National Health Service Corps loan repayment program.
Political assessment: Very safe bill. CHCs have broad support across the aisle and the program has been reauthorised without controversy multiple times. Collins is a strong Republican co-sponsor. Our state has 23 federally qualified health centres that would benefit directly. Good for a “bringing resources home” message.
2. Maternal Health Equity Act
Sponsor: Sen. Booker (D-NJ) / Sen. Murkowski (R-AK)
Status: Reintroduced from last session. Previously passed committee 15–7.

Extends Medicaid postpartum coverage from 60 days to 12 months, creates a $300M grant program for community-based maternal health programmes, and requires HHS to establish standardised maternal mortality review processes. Includes provisions for implicit bias training in OB-GYN residency programmes.
Political assessment: Maternal mortality is a winning issue. Our state’s rates are above the national average, which gives the Senator a clear local angle. The Medicaid extension has some Republican support but the bias training provisions may face pushback. Could potentially be amended to pass more easily.
3. Rural Emergency Medical Services Stabilisation Act
Sponsor: Sen. Tester (D-MT) / Sen. Ernst (R-IA)
Status: Strong bipartisan support. Similar bill passed the Senate last session but died in conference.

Creates a dedicated Medicare payment model for rural EMS agencies, many of which operate at a loss. Provides bridge grants for agencies at imminent risk of closure and establishes a rural EMS training and recruitment pipeline. Also includes provisions for ground ambulance cost reporting.
Political assessment: This is probably our single best bill for the state. We have multiple counties where the nearest ambulance is 40+ minutes away and EMS agencies are closing. The Senator could co-sponsor and make this a signature issue for rural healthcare access. Already has a strong Republican coalition and would play very well in the rural parts of the state where we underperformed last cycle.
4. Drug Pricing Transparency Act
Sponsor: Sen. Warren (D-MA)
Status: Introduced; no Republican co-sponsor. Previous version did not advance out of committee.

Requires pharmaceutical manufacturers to disclose and justify price increases exceeding 10% annually or cumulative increases exceeding 25% over three years. Establishes an HHS review board with authority to flag “unjustified” increases, though without binding price-setting authority. Includes provisions for importing drugs from Canada for certain categories.
Political assessment: Populist appeal and drug pricing polls well in our state. But without Republican support, it’s going nowhere this session. The Senator could co-sponsor for messaging purposes but should understand it won’t pass. Risk of being tagged as “anti-business” by pharma lobby, which has already spent in our state.
5. Fentanyl Response and Community Safety Enhancement Act
Sponsor: Sen. Sinema (I-AZ) / Sen. Cornyn (R-TX)
Status: Strong bipartisan support; fast-tracked in Judiciary but cross-referred to HELP for prevention title.

Omnibus bill combining law enforcement provisions (scheduling of fentanyl analogues, enhanced penalties for trafficking near schools and treatment centres) with a $2.1B public health title covering expanded access to naloxone, medication-assisted treatment, and community recovery services. The HELP committee’s jurisdiction covers the public health title.
Political assessment: Opioid crisis plays extremely well in the state. The Senator can engage specifically on the treatment and recovery provisions, which is substantively more interesting and avoids the “tough on crime vs. public health” binary. Very high likelihood of passage in some form.
6. Telehealth Permanency and Improvement Act
Sponsor: Sen. Wicker (R-MS) / Sen. Schatz (D-HI)
Status: Bipartisan; passed committee last session. Strong industry support.

Makes permanent the telehealth flexibilities introduced during COVID-19, including Medicare reimbursement parity for audio-only visits, removal of originating-site restrictions, and licensure reciprocity for interstate telehealth. Also establishes a $150M grant programme for broadband-connected health infrastructure in rural areas.
Political assessment: Non-controversial and popular. Telehealth is used by a large share of our state’s population and the flexibilities are currently operating under temporary extensions that expire in September. Good “getting things done” bill but not a differentiator — many senators will want credit for this one.
7. AI in Healthcare Safety and Standards Act
Sponsor: Sen. Lujan (D-NM) / Sen. Young (R-IN)
Status: Recently introduced; gaining interest but still early. Referred to HELP.

Directs the FDA to develop a regulatory framework for AI-based clinical decision support tools, requires algorithmic auditing for bias in diagnostic AI deployed in federally funded settings, and creates a $200M ARPA-H programme for research on safe and effective clinical AI. Includes reporting requirements for adverse events linked to AI-assisted diagnosis or treatment.
Political assessment: Emerging issue with some bipartisan appeal. The AI angle is timely and could position the Senator as forward-thinking. But it’s early-stage — the lobbying landscape hasn’t settled, the tech companies haven’t fully engaged, and the FDA is still working through its own guidance. Medium passability this session; more likely to be a positioning bill for next session.
8. Pandemic Preparedness and Response Modernisation Act
Sponsor: Sen. Murray (D-WA) / Sen. Burr (R-NC)
Status: Reintroduced; previous version passed HELP committee 17–5 in the 118th Congress but never received a floor vote. Burr has since retired; Sen. Tillis (R-NC) is considering co-sponsorship but has not committed.

Comprehensive overhaul of US pandemic preparedness infrastructure. The bill has four titles:

Title I — Surveillance and Early Warning: Establishes a national pathogen surveillance network integrating wastewater monitoring, genomic sequencing, and hospital syndromic surveillance into a single real-time dashboard. Requires CDC to maintain standing agreements with state public health labs for surge sequencing capacity. Authorises $1.2B over five years.
Title II — Medical Countermeasure Acceleration: Reforms BARDA’s contracting authority to enable rapid-response development partnerships. Creates a standing “warm base” manufacturing network of at least five domestic facilities capable of producing mRNA, protein subunit, and viral vector vaccines within 90 days of a declared emergency. Estimated authorisation: $3.4B over five years.
Title III — State and Local Capacity: Restores and expands the Public Health Emergency Preparedness (PHEP) cooperative agreement, which has been cut by 30% in real terms since 2003. Requires states to maintain minimum staffing levels for epidemiology and contact tracing as a condition of funding. Includes $800M for modernising state and local health department data systems, many of which still rely on fax-based reporting.

The Center for Health Security estimates that the surveillance, manufacturing, and governance provisions across the four titles would reduce expected mortality from a future pandemic of comparable severity to COVID-19 by more than half.

Political assessment: Pandemic preparedness doesn’t have a natural constituency. Voters aren’t thinking about the next pandemic, and the political environment around COVID — mandates, lockdowns, the WHO — is still toxic enough that anything that sounds like “pandemic infrastructure” triggers resistance on the right. The bill passed committee with a large bipartisan majority last session, which shows it’s not inherently partisan, but floor time is scarce and leadership will prioritise bills with more political urgency. For our state specifically, this isn’t a natural campaign issue. It doesn’t lend itself to ribbon-cutting or a clear “bringing resources home” narrative the way the CHC or Rural EMS bills do.

9. Public Option for ACA Marketplace Exchanges
Sponsor: Sen. Schatz (D-HI) / No Republican co-sponsor
Status: Introduced; zero Republican support. Dead on arrival in current Senate.

Creates a federal public health insurance option available on ACA exchanges, with premiums tied to 110% of Medicare rates. Includes provisions for automatic enrolment of uninsured individuals below 200% FPL who have not selected a plan during open enrolment.
Political assessment: Not passable. Full stop. The Senator could co-sponsor for positioning with the base, but there is zero chance of committee markup, let alone floor action, in this Congress. Recommend avoiding unless the Senator specifically wants to stake out a progressive position for other reasons.
10. Mental Health Parity Enforcement Strengthening Act
Sponsor: Sen. Cassidy (R-LA) / Sen. Murphy (D-CT)
Status: Bipartisan; substantial interest from both sides. Building on the 2024 final rule on parity compliance.

Strengthens enforcement mechanisms for the Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act by requiring insurers to submit annual compliance reports to CMS, establishing a dedicated parity enforcement office within HHS, and creating a private right of action for patients denied mental health coverage in violation of parity requirements. Also increases penalties for non-compliance.
Political assessment: Strong bipartisan support and very safe territory. Mental health is one of the few areas where there’s genuine cross-party energy. Cassidy is a credible Republican lead. Good for our state, where mental health provider shortages are severe. Not a “signature” bill for the Senator but a solid addition to a legislative portfolio.
11. Nursing Workforce Pipeline Act
Sponsor: Sen. Baldwin (D-WI) / Sen. Capito (R-WV)
Status: Bipartisan; passed HELP last session as part of a larger workforce package that stalled.

Increases the cap on Medicare-funded graduate nursing education positions by 10,000 over five years, creates a $500M competitive grant programme for nursing schools to expand capacity, and establishes a loan forgiveness programme for nurses who commit to practicing in underserved areas for at least three years.
Political assessment: Very passable and broadly supported. The nursing shortage is real and felt across the country. Our state has multiple nursing programmes that would benefit from the education grants. Not flashy but substantive and easy to message. Could be included in a larger health workforce package.
12. Medicare Hearing Aid Coverage Act
Sponsor: Sen. Warren (D-MA) / Sen. Grassley (R-IA)
Status: Moderate bipartisan support. CBO score came in higher than expected, which has cooled some enthusiasm.

Adds hearing aid coverage to Medicare Part B, including fitting, follow-up, and replacement every five years. Builds on the 2022 OTC hearing aid rule by covering the clinical-grade devices and audiological services that the OTC market doesn’t address.
Political assessment: Plays well with seniors, who are a key voting bloc in our state. Grassley’s involvement gives it Republican credibility. But the CBO score ($8.2B over 10 years) is a sticking point — some moderates have backed away since the score came out. Could pass if attached to a larger Medicare package but unlikely as a standalone this session.
13. Veteran Caregiver Support Expansion Act
Sponsor: Sen. Duckworth (D-IL) / Sen. Moran (R-KS)
Status: Strong bipartisan support. Veterans’ Affairs Committee has primary jurisdiction but HELP has a referral on the respite care provisions.

Expands the VA’s Program of Comprehensive Assistance for Family Caregivers to include caregivers of veterans with severe mental health conditions (currently limited primarily to physical injuries). Increases the stipend and adds respite care, mental health support for caregivers, and job training. Also creates a pilot programme for community-based respite networks.
Political assessment: Veterans’ issues are always politically safe and this is a particularly sympathetic bill. Strong bipartisan coalition. Our state has a significant veteran population. The Senator could co-sponsor without spending much political capital. The main question is whether it’s distinctive enough to be a “priority” vs. a reliable co-sponsorship.

Preliminary Recommendation
Based on the intersection of passability, state impact, and political positioning, my initial shortlist would be:

1. Rural EMS Stabilisation Act — Highest passability, strongest state play, clear rural healthcare narrative.
2. Fentanyl Response Enhancement Act — High passability, strong state salience, bipartisan.
3. Maternal Health Equity Act or Community Health Center Expansion — Either would round out the portfolio with a healthcare access bill that has a good chance of passing and plays well in the state.

Happy to discuss. I know the Senator may also want to consider the mental health parity or telehealth bills — both are strong on substance and passability, just less distinctive as a priority claim.
